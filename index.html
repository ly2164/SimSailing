Sailing Physics Simulator — Build History

Each release adds a new version file; only the badge at the top of this page changes. Older versions are kept forever, so links that were shared never break. V0.1–V0.4 keep their original Chinese interface; V0.5 onward are English.
V1.0 Control Tolerance Current EN
Two handling improvements. Grab tolerance: a tap no longer has to land on the 4 px track — anything within 50 px of a control takes hold of it, nearest one wins. Axis tolerance: once a control is grabbed the drag keeps driving it along its own axis even when the pointer wanders off the track, so a sloppy diagonal swipe still trims the sail and the knob follows. Mouse drags used to break the moment the cursor left the slider. Verified 17 of 17 scenarios, including every V0.9 rudder release path.

V0.9 Rudder Release Fix EN
Makes rudder self-centering reliable. It previously only listened for touchend and a document mouseup, so the rudder stayed hard over whenever the drag ended any other way: a cancelled touch (incoming call, notification, browser reclaiming the gesture), the page losing focus, or the tab being hidden. Adds touchcancel, blur and visibilitychange to one shared release path, and holds off resetting while a second finger is still down. Verified 11 of 12 release scenarios; a mouse released entirely outside the browser window delivers no event to the page and cannot be detected.

V0.8 Bounded Leeway Model EN
Replaces the unbounded drift accumulator with a capped leeway angle. The old model amplified sideways speed about 32×, reaching 72.8° of leeway at beam reach — the hull would slide almost bodily sideways. Real hulls settle in a band because sideways resistance grows with slip. Leeway is now 38° max with the centreboard raised and 12° with it down, worst just above the no-go edge and falling to zero dead downwind. With the board up the boat now crabs upwind at 45° instead of drifting south, which is what a boardless hull actually does.

V0.7 Leeway Direction Fix EN
Fixes an inverted leeway sign that pushed the boat into the wind. The drift term was being subtracted from y while x added it, so the boat slid upwind at every heading — point it east under a north wind and it crawled north instead of sliding south. Regression introduced during the V0.3 minification and present through V0.6; V0.1–V0.2 were correct. Verified 0 of 296 headings with upwind leeway.

V0.6 Realistic Sailing Polar EN
Replaces the analytic speed curve with a measured dinghy polar. Upwind progress is now realistic: 45° off the wind gives 62% of top speed instead of 95%, top speed sits at beam reach (90°), and dead downwind is slower than a reach. Best velocity made good toward the wind lands at 47.5° making 43.9% of top speed. Also fixes a discontinuity where side force jumped from 0.05 to 1.0 at 90°.
