Why it will not sail into the wind

Inside 32° of the wind the sail cannot push the hull forward at all — the shaded wedge on the diagram. That is the no-go zone. A boat has to leave it by tacking: sail across at about 45°, then turn through the wind and repeat. The best you can do head-to-wind is the velocity made good, the upwind figure in the readout.

Sail → drive

The sail is sheeted to leeward and the best angle changes with the point of sail: tight in when close-hauled, eased right out when running. Trim error shows how far off that ideal you are, and efficiency is what it costs you. Trim badly and the boat simply goes slower.

Rudder → turn

The blade deflects water, swinging the stern one way and the bow the other. Turn rate scales with speed and with how much the board is resisting, and it falls away to almost nothing when the boat is stopped — which is exactly why a boat caught in irons cannot be steered out.

Centreboard → leeway

Sideways force makes the hull slide off to leeward; the board is what resists it. Lower it and leeway drops from tens of degrees to a few, so more of your speed points where the bow points. Raise it and the boat crabs sideways — faster-looking, but you lose the direction.
